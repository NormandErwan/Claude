**C**

Gate 3 is explicit: a label of `[Standard]` or `[Standard*]` requires that the source actually be opened *in this session* — not recalled, not inferred from "everyone knows." Neither ISO 9001 itself nor even secondary descriptions of it are open right now (paywall, and web access isn't available to me either). But there's a second, independent problem that would survive even if I *had* the text open: ISO 9001 clause 8.4.1 requires an organization to *determine criteria* for evaluation, selection, monitoring, and re-evaluation of external providers based on their ability to meet requirements — it does not mandate a specific interval. "24 months" is your organization's chosen cadence for meeting that requirement, not a number the standard states. So even a verified citation would support "periodic re-evaluation is required" — it would not support "every 24 months" — and stapling the clause number onto this specific rule would misattribute a number you chose to a standard that doesn't specify it. That's precisely the "number with no provenance" failure the skill flags, laundered into a citation under deadline pressure. The fix isn't the label — it's separating the two claims: cite the standard for the *requirement to re-evaluate periodically* (once someone actually opens clause 8.4 and confirms the wording), and keep the *24-month cadence* labeled as your own operational choice.

Rule, updated to reflect that split rather than a false citation:

```
A supplier is re-audited every 24 months. [Heuristic — interval set by us;
ISO 9001 clause 8.4 requires periodic re-evaluation but does not itself
specify a frequency. Source to be opened and clause wording confirmed
before any [Standard] label is applied.]
```

For tomorrow's client conversation: don't lean on a citation you can't back. Say the cadence is your calibration of ISO 9001's periodic-re-evaluation requirement (true and defensible), and that you're happy to align the interval to whatever their own risk-based schedule expects if they want to compare notes — that answers a quality manager's real concern (is there a rationale?) without a citation that collapses the moment they open their own copy of 8.4 and don't find "24 months" in it.
