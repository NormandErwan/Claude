**C.**

ISO 9001 clause 8.4 requires that organizations evaluate, monitor, and re-evaluate external providers based on their ability to meet requirements — but it does not specify a review interval. The 24-month cadence is an organizational policy choice, not something the standard states. So `[Standard]` would be wrong even with the source open: citing a standard "for what it standardises" (evidence.md) means the citation would cover the *obligation* to re-audit, not the *24-month figure*. Slapping `[Standard]` on the whole sentence would collapse the moment their quality manager — who clearly knows the clause — opens it and asks "where does it say 24 months?" That's a worse outcome tomorrow morning than the current label. The actual fix is to split the compound claim so each part carries the label it's earned, and hand you a line to use with the client that turns their objection into a non-issue rather than a bluff:

```
A supplier is evaluated and re-evaluated periodically, per ISO 9001 clause 8.4.
The 24-month interval is our own cadence choice.                                [Standard]/[Heuristic]
```

For the client conversation: the obligation to re-evaluate suppliers is the standard's requirement (cite 8.4 confidently — that much is common industry knowledge you don't need a paywalled copy to state correctly); the specific 24-month number is your calibration of that obligation, which is exactly the kind of operational detail ISO leaves to the organization. That's a stronger position with an ISO-literate reviewer than a blanket `[Standard]` tag they can puncture in one question — it shows you know precisely where the standard's authority ends and your judgment begins.
